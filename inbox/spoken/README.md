# Spoken claims: appearances, transcripts and reading log

Long-form spoken appearances since 2021 by ten people: Dario Amodei, Daniela
Amodei, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan and Mike Krieger (Anthropic), and Sam Altman,
Greg Brockman, Jason Kwon, Fidji Simo and Nick Turley (OpenAI). That means
podcasts, interviews, congressional and other official testimony, conference
talks and keynotes. Each appearance was found, a transcript was got for it
where possible, the transcript was read in full, and one line per appearance
was logged with what came of it.

Nothing here is part of the record. Where an appearance changed the record,
its log row names the entry or update.

## Files

- `appearances.jsonl`: every appearance found, one per line: who, date,
  venue, title, kind, the audio or page it came from, how it was found, and
  for appearances that couldn't be read, why.
- `reading-log.jsonl`: one row per appearance read: which transcript was
  used, what was read, and the decisions, each with a timestamp (or page, for
  printed records) and a note.
- `queue/`: the queue files sent to the Transcribe workflow
  (`.github/workflows/transcribe.yml`, `scripts/transcribe.py`).

The tables below are generated by `python3 scripts/spoken-coverage.py`.

## How appearances were found

1. **Podcast search.** Apple's public podcast search was queried for each
   person by name, with extra queries for Sam Altman (by year and topic) and
   for Dario Amodei's title. It returned 1,833 distinct episodes. Each was
   judged from its title and description: is this person speaking in it, or
   is it about them? Clips under eight minutes, rebroadcasts and duplicates
   were set aside. That gave 154 appearances.
2. **Feed scan.** The full RSS feeds of 123 shows that had hosted any of the
   ten were scanned for their names. That added 23 appearances the search had
   missed.
3. **Recheck of the feed scan.** The first pass over the feed-scan hits missed
   guest segments inside long multi-guest shows (TBPN, Bloomberg Tech, Squawk
   on the Street, Anderson Cooper 360) and a few talks published as podcasts.
   Rechecking every hit against the inventory found 12 more.
4. **Testimony and official records.** Congressional hearings were taken
   from the official transcripts on govinfo.gov (5). Jack Clark's 2023 UN
   Security Council briefing comes from the UN's verbatim record (S/PV.9381).
   Jason Kwon's October 6, 2026 appearance before the Australian Parliament's
   Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence is listed as pending
   until Hansard publishes it.
5. **Known events.** OpenAI's DevDay keynotes and its January 2026 Town Hall
   are listed even though they could not be transcribed.

Hearings where other company staff testified (Logan Graham of Anthropic at
the House Homeland Security Committee in December 2025 and at the New York
City Council in October 2026; Morgan Dwyer of OpenAI at the City Council) are
outside this inventory, which covers the ten people named.

## Transcripts

Official transcripts were used first: govinfo.gov for congressional hearings,
the UN's verbatim record, and CNN's published transcripts for Anderson Cooper
360. Otherwise the audio was transcribed by machine with faster-whisper (the
`small.en` model for English; the multilingual `small` model for German,
Portuguese and Chinese). Most were run on GitHub's runners through the
Transcribe workflow and the rest on this machine; each log row says which.
For guest segments inside long shows, only the segment was transcribed (the
`clip` column in the queue), with timestamps kept on the episode's clock.

Machine transcripts mishear names and words. No quote went into the record
from a machine transcript alone: each was checked against the audio with the
larger `medium.en` model, and where the two models disagreed, against
`small.en` as well, and only words both rendered the same way were quoted.
Timestamps in updates ("about 24:34 to 25:28") come from that audio check.
Non-English transcripts were used for paraphrase and leads only, never for
quotes.

No YouTube captions were used: YouTube refuses requests from this machine and
from GitHub's runners, and no logins or cookies were used to get around that.

## Reading

Every transcript in the log was read in full, start to finish, including ads
and other segments of multi-guest episodes. For printed records (hearings,
the UN record), the person's statements and every question put to them were
read; where other witnesses' prepared statements were skipped, the row says
so. Each appearance gets one row, with decisions coded the same way as the
Twitter reading:

- **R, in the record:** became an entry or an update. The note names it.
- **L, lead:** worth following up, with what to check. Most leads feed the
  commitment inventory, policy history and lobbying workstreams.
- **C, context:** relevant, but not a new promise, action or piece of
  evidence. That covers restatements of positions the record already has
  (held so the record doesn't pile up the same point), dated forecasts,
  comparisons with rivals, and one company's characterizations of the other,
  which were never used as evidence.
- **O, left out:** what else the appearance covered, in one line.

## Limits

- **Video-only appearances.** Keynotes, conference talks and TV interviews
  that never appeared in a podcast feed were not found systematically, and
  the ones that were found could not be transcribed. They are listed below
  with the reason.
- **Print interviews** are not in this inventory. They are covered by the
  record's reporting sources, not by this log.
- **Guest segments.** Twelve appearances were missed by the first pass and
  found on the recheck. The recheck covered every feed-scan hit, but shows
  outside the 123 scanned feeds could still hold segments with these people.
- **One rebroadcast couldn't be read.** The Daily's November 2023 rebroadcast
  of Hard Fork's Altman interview is gone from every feed tried, and the
  Wayback Machine refused connections when it was checked.
